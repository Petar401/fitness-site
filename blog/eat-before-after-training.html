What to eat before and after training

By Jordan Hale · 22 Mar 2026 · 5 min read

Workout nutrition gets wildly overcomplicated. You don't need a cupboard full of powders and timed shakes. For 95% of people, a couple of simple principles around real food will do everything you need to train well and recover quickly. Here's the no-nonsense version.

Before training

The goal is steady energy without feeling heavy or sluggish. Eat a balanced meal with some carbs and protein around 2–3 hours before, or a lighter snack 30–60 minutes before if you're short on time.

  • 2–3 hours before: chicken & rice, eggs on toast, or a chicken/tofu wrap.
  • 30–60 mins before: a banana, a pot of yoghurt, or a slice of toast with honey.
  • Training fasted in the morning? That's fine too — just keep sessions a touch shorter and eat well afterwards.
The best pre-workout meal is one that sits comfortably and gives you energy. Test a few and see what your body likes.

After training

Post-workout, you want protein to support recovery and some carbs to refuel. The old "30-minute anabolic window" panic is overblown — what matters most is getting a good protein-rich meal in within a few hours.

  • Aim for ~25–40g protein: chicken, salmon, eggs, Greek yoghurt, tofu or a protein shake if convenient.
  • Add carbs to refuel: rice, potatoes, pasta, oats or fruit.
  • Rehydrate: water is plenty for most sessions; add a pinch of salt if you've sweated heavily.

Do you need supplements?

Mostly, no. Whey or vegan protein powder is a handy convenience, and creatine is one of the few supplements with strong evidence behind it — but neither is essential. Food first, always. Get the basics from my fat-loss habits guide dialled in before worrying about anything fancy.

Keep it simple

Eat real food, prioritise protein, fuel up sensibly around sessions and stay hydrated. Do that consistently and your training will take care of the rest. If you'd like a plan tailored to your goals and tastes, that's exactly what I do.
