Fat loss for busy professionals: 5 habits that actually work

By Jordan Hale · 28 Apr 2026 · 7 min read

Most of my Norwich clients aren't training for a competition — they're juggling demanding jobs, family and a social life, and they just want to feel leaner, stronger and more energetic. The good news? You don't need hours in the gym or a miserable diet. You need a handful of high-impact habits done consistently. Here are the five that do almost all the work.

1. Anchor your day around protein

Protein keeps you full, protects muscle while you lose fat, and is the hardest thing to overeat. Aim for a palm-sized portion at every meal — eggs or Greek yoghurt at breakfast, chicken, fish, tofu or beans at lunch and dinner. Nail this one habit and your appetite becomes far easier to manage.

2. Walk more than you think you need to

Steps are the most underrated fat-loss tool there is. They burn meaningful energy without making you ravenous the way hard cardio can. A target of 8,000–10,000 steps a day — a walk along the Riverside, parking further away, a lunchtime loop — adds up fast.

Consistency at 80% beats perfection at 100% that you can't sustain past Tuesday.

3. Lift weights 2–3 times a week

Strength training is what shapes your body as the fat comes off. Two or three short, focused sessions a week is plenty — and far more effective than endless cardio. New to it? Start with my beginner's strength plan.

4. Build a "minimum viable" routine

On your busiest weeks, have a fallback you can't talk yourself out of: a 20-minute full-body session and a 6,000-step floor. Lowering the bar on chaotic days keeps the streak alive — and the streak is what gets results.

5. Plan for real life — including the pub

Norwich has great restaurants and a brilliant social scene, and your plan has to survive contact with them. Eat a bit lighter earlier in the day, lead with protein and veg, and enjoy yourself without guilt. Sustainable fat loss includes the things you love.

The honest truth

Fat loss isn't complicated, but it is easier with accountability. Having someone in your corner — checking in, adjusting the plan, keeping you honest — is the difference-maker for most busy people. That's exactly what my coaching is built for.
